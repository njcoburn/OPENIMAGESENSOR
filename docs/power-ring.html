Physical supply ring and sensor power routing

Built: a 1.110 × 1.010 mm development ring with two supply bond pads, four foundry corners, and 125 filler cells. The unchanged 3×3 sensor is placed inside and its power and ground are routed. The bottom two pads are VDD and ground; the central block is the sensor. Signal pads and their routing are still absent.

The final routes use 4 µm trunks and bridges matching the existing sensor buses. Their total extracted resistance is 12.34158 Ω, versus 27.1802 Ω for the initial narrow routes. A new via was moved outside the existing dummy-fill boundary to clear width/spacing violations. The sensor GDS and foundry macro devices are unchanged.

More than two clamps: the corners add eight clamp circuits, giving ten total. Each filler includes 32 MOS-capacitor units, and all 125 instances are included in the schematic. This changes startup loading compared with the earlier two-pad experiment.

Supply-load test: explicit model scope

This test uses the installed ring schematic models plus RC extracted from the actual isolated connecting wires. It includes a 0.5 Ω / 2 nH board feed, 100 nF decoupling with ESR/ESL, a 33 kΩ core load and 100 µA load pulses. It does not yet include ring-metal resistance, complete assembly coupling, or a new camera-image scan.

This does not relax the earlier pixel simulation settings. Extracted wire resistors remain at their nominal values in the temperature tests; metal temperature coefficients are not yet included.

Hot time-step cross-check: maximum supply difference 0.0352 µV and ground difference 0.0000 µV on a common 100 ns grid; both below 10 µV. Strict direct and exact-interface diagnostic runs both timed out at 900 seconds; no strict-tolerance pass is claimed.

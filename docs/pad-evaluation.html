Analog pad evaluation: leakage, bias and sampling

Functional circuit candidate: 5/5 selected imaging cases pass with a finite-transition ADC model. The installed gf180mcu_fd_io__asig_5p0 was evaluated on BIAS, PREF, VRESET and BUF. The extracted sensor GDS is unchanged.

Leakage and capacitance

36 isolated-pad conditions cover three diode sections, four temperatures (−40, 27, 85, 125 °C) and three supplies (3.0, 3.3, 3.6 V). Signal capacitance is differential AC capacitance at 1 kHz with ideal AC-grounded supply rails; it excludes bond wires and metal extraction. The pad's rail MOS capacitor uses its typical section. Candidate secondary protection

Each pin uses a 100 Ω ideal series resistor, with a diode pair at the core side: 3.3 V N+/substrate and P+/well junctions, each 12 µm² area and 26 µm perimeter. For a physical implementation the resistor must become an appropriate poly resistor. The foundry documentation calls for local secondary protection; the tested dimensions alone do not establish ESD robustness or thin-oxide protection.

All 84 DC conditions converge for each of the pad-only and pad-plus-secondary candidates. Relative to matching unpadded resistor-bias cases, the secondary candidate changes the column reference by at most 0.0144% and the buffer reference by at most 0.1799%. Actual current entering the core is measured after the protection network, excluding its leakage.

Sampling and numerical checks

Worst HOLD error across the five finite-switch secondary-protection cases: 0.217 mV, compared with the 0.5 mV screen. The tests retain the generic 20 pF switched ADC sampling capacitor, 100 pF board load, 100 Ω board isolation resistor and bond-wire model. The old assumed pad capacitance is replaced with device models plus a separate 1 pF external allowance at each bias pin and at the output package node. VRESET remains an ideal ramped 2 V external source.

All five secondary-protection runs with the original discontinuous ideal ADC switches failed with timestep-too-small errors. These failed decks and logs are retained. The successful runs replace the ADC switches with smooth conductance transitions (100 Ω on, 1 TΩ off; tanh control width 0.1 V over the existing 10 ns control edges). This is still a behavioral ADC load, not a selected ADC's transistor model.

Two matched pad-only controls change sampled output by at most 0.00002 mV when switching to the finite-transition model. The hot secondary case rerun at 0.05 µs and tighter relative tolerance gives 0.216 mV HOLD error and changes sampled output by at most 0.00097 mV.

Decision: retain this analog-pad/protection topology as a candidate for physical implementation. Next resolve the pad-ring rails and clamps, digital-input voltage compatibility, realizable poly resistor and local diode layout, then rerun pad-inclusive DRC/LVS and extraction. ESD pulses, clamp effectiveness, supply inductance/coupling, powered-input/unpowered-core cases, resistor process variation, photoleakage and assembly contamination are not qualified here.
